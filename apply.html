This is the third and final NETGAIN call for PhD project proposals. We expect to fund about 20 studentships across St Andrews, Glasgow, Aberdeen and Durham, starting in academic year 2027/28.

Supervisory team

Proposals come from a team of three to five supervisors, which must include:

  • academics from at least two of the four partner universities
  • one named individual from an external partner organisation
  • one academic with demonstrated expertise in ecology or environmental science
  • one academic with demonstrated expertise in another NETGAIN-relevant discipline

What to submit

Project summary (up to 400 words)
Explicitly defined objectives and scope.
Relevance statement (up to 300 words)
How the project fits the NETGAIN vision.
Interdisciplinarity and co-creation (up to 300 words)
The project's collaborative aspects and what each supervisor contributes.

Proposals are submitted through the online NETGAIN proposal submission form, linked from the full call document below. The Word template is there to help you draft your answers before submitting. Questions to netgain@st-andrews.ac.uk.

Widening participation pathway

This pathway exists to address historical inequalities and make NETGAIN and its partner universities more inclusive. It targets barriers that limit competitiveness because of unequal access to opportunity, not differences in ability. Our aim is for 30% of NETGAIN students to be recruited through it.

Who is eligible

  • You are eligible for a Home award under UKRI rules. The scheme is not open to international applicants.
  • You identify as BAME, and/or your childhood (under 18) or school postcode in the UK falls in POLAR4 quintile 1 or 2.
  • You meet the standard entry requirements for a PhD: normally an undergraduate degree, with a master's degree desirable.

How it works

This is an open call for expressions of interest from prospective students and from supervisors. The NETGAIN team works with you to develop a project across the network, including introductions to supervisors at partner universities and to external organisations. Projects must show the same scientific rigour, interdisciplinarity and co-creation as every NETGAIN PhD, but candidates are assessed through a separate pathway rather than the standard competition, and eligible applicants are invited to an interview preparation webinar.

Industry-sponsored pathway

For professionals already working in a relevant sector who want to undertake a doctorate without leaving their job. A sponsoring employer funds the studentship in full or in part, the candidate stays in employment throughout, and the research is co-designed by the candidate, the employer and academic supervisors so that it is both rigorous and relevant to the organisation's priorities.

Who is eligible

  • You are eligible for a Home award under UKRI rules. The scheme is not open to international applicants.
  • You meet the standard entry requirements for a PhD: normally an undergraduate degree, with a master's degree desirable.
  • Your employer is willing to sponsor the studentship and to take part in supervision.

How it works

Study can be part-time, at a minimum of 0.5 FTE, to fit around work. Students are co-supervised by academic and industry staff and are embedded in the NETGAIN training and research environment alongside the rest of their cohort. Employers interested in sponsoring a studentship can also read about directly funded research.
